The pause button nobody has to press.

Measured

Numbers, not adjectives.

How it works

Five steps, and the last one is a transaction.

  1. The protocol writes a red line in plain language and funds a bounty behind it.
  2. Anyone, or a bot, sees the line being crossed and raises an alarm with a deposit.
  3. The validators read the evidence, and read the protocol's own state to check it.
  4. They have to reach the same verdict, or nothing happens and nothing moves.
  5. Withdrawals stop in that same transaction. A false alarm loses its deposit.

The shape

Lines of the same kind.

The line above was run on chain. These are the same kind of sentence: everybody understands them, none of them compiles, and every one is enforced today by somebody watching a dashboard who then has to reach a multisig.

  • A lending poolNo single actor may take more than a tenth of the pool in an hour, however many addresses it borrows through.
  • A grant treasuryNo recipient may be paid twice for the same piece of work under two names.
  • A market makerLiquidity may not be pulled in concert by the providers who added it together.

Design

Two decisions worth arguing with.

The integration at the top of this page is the whole of what a protocol adds: no inheritance, no proxy, no upgrade, no key handed to anybody. These two choices behind it are the ones people push back on.

It fails open on purpose.

A guardian that cannot be reached does not stop you. Failing closed would make the guard the single point of failure for every protocol trusting it, so one bad deploy of ours would freeze all of them. A broken guard protects nothing, which is the world before you adopted it.

Anyone may raise it, one party may lower it.

The owner can lower a guard only after a hold, because a guard that could be lifted as fast as it went up is a race between two transactions, and an attacker sends transactions faster than a person does.

Limits

What it will not do.

It does not find exploits.

Somebody has to see it and say so. What this removes is the wait between the seeing and the stopping, which is where the money usually goes.

It judges what is happening, never what might.

A theoretical bug, a design somebody dislikes, a risk with no event behind it: all refused, and refused at the cost of the deposit. A pause button anyone can press with a paragraph is a denial of service with extra steps.

It does not stop an atomic exploit.

Nothing outside the transaction can. This is built for the drains that take minutes and hours, which is most of them by value.
