The pause button
nobody has to press.

A protocol publishes a red line in plain language and funds a bounty. Anyone who finds it being crossed says so, with evidence and a deposit. The validators read the evidence themselves, and the protocol stops in that same transaction.

The line

A rule with no implementation.

There is a number in it, and a contract could check that part perfectly well. What no contract can work out is who the number applies to, because an actor is not a field in any ledger.

How it works

Five steps, and the last one is a transaction.

    plain language, funded bounty

  2. 02

    Raise an alarm

    evidence, deposit at stake

  3. 03

  4. 04

    They have to agree

    or nothing moves at all

  5. 05

    Halt triggered

Autonomy

Nobody was watching either of these.

The agent was given a blunt second look: two addresses funded near each other, withdrawing near each other, more than a third of the vault between them. That is a coincidence, not a finding, and it says so in the evidence it writes. Then it was pointed at two vaults it had never seen.

Same agent, same rule, same evidence template, no human in either run. It was right once and wrong once, and it paid for the wrong one.

Before publishing any of this we went looking for the ways it could be wrong, then handed the whole record to an outside reviewer and asked it to attack rather than approve. One break we found ourselves. The other we did not.

Limits

What it will not do.

Limit 01

It does not find exploits.

Somebody has to see it and say so, and that somebody can be a bot. What this removes is the wait between the seeing and the stopping: the alarm, the reading and the halt are one transaction, so there is no key to reach and nobody to wake at four in the morning. The seeing is still yours.

Limit 02

It judges what is, never what might be.

A theoretical bug, a design somebody dislikes, a risk with no event behind it: all refused, and refused at the cost of the deposit. Ten false alarms out of ten were turned away, including a rule nobody published and an instruction hidden in the evidence. A pause button anyone can press with a paragraph is a denial of service with extra steps.

Limit 03

It does not stop an atomic exploit.

Nothing outside the transaction can, and pretending otherwise would be the easiest lie on this page. This is built for the drains that take minutes and hours, which is where most of the value has actually gone: Nomad ran 960 transactions, Curve emptied three pools over two hours.

A rule that cannot be written in code, held by a network that reads it.
