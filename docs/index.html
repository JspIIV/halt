The pause button
nobody has to press

A protocol publishes a red line in plain language and funds a bounty. Anyone who finds it being crossed says so, with evidence and a deposit. The validators weigh that one claim against four things its author did not write: the protocol’s own report, its movement record, the balance the guard reads off the chain, and a witness the protocol named in advance. If the line is crossed, the protocol stops in that same transaction.

How it works

            
How a halt happens

            

                From a line published in advance to a protocol stopped in one transaction. Whoever
                raises the alarm writes only the claim; everything it is weighed against is read from
                the protocol and the chain, in the same transaction that judges it.
            

        

        

            
        

    


    
    

        

            
What a protocol adds

            
Three things, and two of them are the point

            

                A protocol does not hand the guard a key or inherit anything. It adds a check and,
                to make a claim about it answerable, it exposes what it knows.
            

        


        

            

                
01  CHECK THE GUARD

                
Ask before it pays

                

                    Four lines in the withdrawal path, calling halted()
                    on the guardian. No key is handed over, and the only thing given up is the right to keep
                    paying out while a guard is up. It fails open: a guardian it cannot reach does not freeze it.
                

            


            

                
02  REPORT ITS OWN BOOK

                
A ledger, not just totals

                

                    Two views: status() for the totals and
                    entries() for what actually moved, in order.
                    This is what lets a claim about particular withdrawals be checked against the record instead
                    of believed. With only totals, a true alarm naming figures the protocol never reports cannot
                    be corroborated, and is refused.
                

            


            

                
03  NAME A WITNESS

                
Its own agent, on the record

                

                    Optionally, name_witness() points the guard at an
                    agent the protocol runs over its own data, before any alarm. It can convict and it cannot
                    acquit: the round is told to trust it when it agrees with the accuser and to discount it when
                    it agrees with the protocol, so a protocol cannot talk its way out through its own monitor.
                

            

        


        

            contracts/monitor.py is a working witness in about a hundred lines:
            per address, in and out, and the largest share taken inside the window. Arithmetic, no model.

Limits

            
What it will not do

        


        

            

                
It does not find exploits.

                

                    Somebody has to see it and say so. What this removes is the wait between the seeing and the stopping.
                

            

            

                
It judges what is, never what might be.

                

                    A theoretical bug or a risk with no event behind it is refused at the cost of the deposit.
                

            

            

                
It does not stop an atomic exploit.

                

                    Nothing outside the transaction can. This is built for drains that take minutes and hours.
