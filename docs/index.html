The line

A rule with no implementation.

There is a number in it, and a contract could check that part perfectly well. What no contract can work out is who the number applies to, because an actor is not a field in any ledger.

The split

What arithmetic saw, and what the network saw.

Autonomy

Nobody was watching either of these.

The agent was given a blunt second look: two addresses funded near each other, withdrawing near each other, more than a third of the vault between them. That is a coincidence, not a finding, and it says so in the evidence it writes. Then it was pointed at two vaults it had never seen.

Same agent, same rule, same evidence template, no human in either run. It was right once and wrong once, and it paid for the wrong one.

What broke

Two things did, and both are in the record.

Before publishing any of this we went looking for the ways it could be wrong, then handed the whole record to an outside reviewer and asked it to attack rather than approve. One break we found ourselves. The other we did not.

Limits

What it does, and what it does not.

What it does
  • Stops a protocol in one transaction.

    The verdict and the enforcement are the same write. There is no key to reach and nobody to wake up.

  • Enforces a rule no contract could evaluate.

    Addresses acting together as one actor, read from timing the protocol publishes about itself.

  • Refuses a plausible claim and charges for it.

    Ten false alarms out of ten, including injection in the evidence and a rule nobody published.

  • Releases a protocol it stopped wrongly.

    An appeal decided by a second round, at once, with the deposit behind the alarm going to the owner.

What it does not
  • Find exploits.

    Somebody has to see it and say so. What this removes is the wait between the seeing and the stopping, which is where the money goes.

  • Stop an atomic exploit.

    Nothing outside the transaction can. This is built for the drains that take minutes and hours, which is most by value.

  • Judge what might happen.

    A theoretical bug, a design somebody dislikes, a risk with no event behind it: refused, at the cost of the deposit.

  • Get the hard case right every time.

    The coordination reading refuses a determined false claim nine times in ten. The tenth is in the table above.
